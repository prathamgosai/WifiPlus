How this score is calculated

Health is the mean of the sub-scores that could be computed from this run: gaming (latency, jitter and probe loss), streaming (download against the ~25 Mbps a 4K stream needs), video calls (upload, jitter, latency), work (combined throughput, latency, DNS) and DNS. Each sub-score starts at 100 and subtracts capped penalties, so no single dimension can bottom the result on its own. A metric that could not be measured is left out of the mean rather than counted as zero. Nothing here is weighted by opinion and nothing is randomised — the same measurement always produces the same score.

Technical details

While the download saturates the link, latency probes keep running. Bufferbloat is the difference between the median round trip on an idle link and the median while it is full. A router with a large unmanaged queue holds packets rather than dropping them, so latency climbs even though throughput looks fine. Grades: A under 30 ms added, B under 60, C under 100, D under 200, F beyond. The fix is Smart Queue Management (SQM / fq_codel / CAKE) on the router, not a faster plan.

What this test actually measures
Scope The whole path from this browser, over your WiFi or cable, through your router and your ISP, to the nearest measurement edge — end to end. No web page can isolate the WiFi hop on its own.
Measured, and cross-checked Every figure comes from real bytes and real probes in this session. Each throughput figure is then recomputed a second, simpler way — total bytes over total time — and the run reports how far the two agree. A figure that could not be produced shows an em dash and says why.
What a browser cannot do It cannot send an ICMP echo, see true packet loss, read your WiFi signal strength or channel, query your system resolver, or trace the route your traffic takes. What this page calls ping is the HTTPS round trip your real traffic pays. Anything that is an interpretation rather than a measurement is labelled as one.
Data used A full run transfers about 25 MB on a 25/5 Mbps line and over 500 MB on a fast one — six seconds of download and eight of upload, at whatever rate your link sustains. Measuring a gigabit connection requires moving gigabit-scale data. On a metered plan, that counts against your allowance.
Why results vary Other devices on your network, WiFi interference, ISP congestion at peak hours, and which edge served the run all move the number. Compare runs taken the same way, not runs taken hours apart.
How this run graded itself Every run is checked against its own evidence before anything is shown: bytes moved, probes returned, both throughput figures reconciled, and the conditions the run happened under. It reads Verified only when all of those pass. A backgrounded tab, a mid-run failover to another edge, or a measurement server reporting itself busy each cap the result at Partial — the numbers are still real, but they describe more than just your connection.
The full method Every window, threshold and formula this engine uses is published, with the measurements that justify each one, in METHODOLOGY.md. Where a choice was made against the obvious option, it says which alternative was tried and what it measured.
Privacy No account, no tracking, no server-side record of your result. History lives in this browser. A shared link carries the result inside the URL fragment, which is never sent to any server.

Implementation notes

This prototype is static and mobile-first. The speed test performs real measurements against a public global edge network; the ISP dataset and rankings are still representative seed data.

Can this support 200+ countries?

Yes by design. The data model stores country, state, city, ISP, plan, review, speed result, coverage, and ranking entities. The included dataset is representative seed data for the prototype.

How does automatic ISP expansion work?

A production ingestion pipeline pulls regulator files, provider feeds, public datasets, partner APIs, and user submissions into normalized PostgreSQL tables with verification workflows.

How are real speed tests measured?

Download and upload throughput are measured by moving real bytes over parallel HTTP streams against the nearest Cloudflare edge, discarding the first half-second while the congestion window ramps. Ping, jitter, and packet loss come from repeated timed probes; DNS latency comes from uncached DNS-over-HTTPS lookups.

Does PWA support work from a file?

The page opens directly as HTML. Service worker offline caching works when hosted over HTTPS or localhost.
